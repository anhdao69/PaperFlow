The RSI Tax: When Recursive Self-Improvement Is Not Worth the Cost

Yitong Zhang, Ang Li, Yisen Wang

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) seeks to improve an agent using its own task experience. Its practical value depends on whether performance gains justify the full cost of self-improvement and task execution. We evaluate this trade-off on continual task streams, scoring each task before its feedback is used for subsequent updates. We define the \emph{RSI Tax} as the token cost of the self-improvement process and include it alongside task-execution costs in end-to-end evaluation. Across six methods and two language models, a bounded-note baseline matches or exceeds the observed accuracy of Meta-Harness, EvoSkill, and RRSI, with mean total-token savings of 65.6-71.0\%. We decompose the RSI Tax into verification, exploration, and writing costs, and track subsequent use of the associated state or artifact. For these three RSI methods, verification accounts for 74.8--93.6% of the aggregate RSI Tax. A substantial share of the RSI Tax is also associated with states or artifacts that are not used later in the evaluated streams. These results expose a practical limitation of costly RSI procedures: improvements in the deployed agent need not justify the expense of the improvement process. They motivate RSI objectives that jointly optimize the RSI Tax and expected task-execution cost while ensuring continued growth in agent capabilities across the task stream.

Domain-Routed Delta Optimization for Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation

Lin Yao

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation assigns each sample a domain teacher but typically updates shared student parameters. We introduce Domain-Routed Delta Optimization (DRD-MOPD), which specifies which parameter components each domain's samples update. The student is the sum of a frozen base and trainable components initialized from expert-minus-base weights. All components participate in every forward pass; four update modes define the correspondence between sample domains and components, each with independent optimizer states. We analyze these update rules and measure retention of the original expert geometry through angles, projection coefficients, and energy. Prior work such as Open-MOPD distills directly from the base model; 97.02% of its released final checkpoint's increment energy lies outside the span of the original expert directions. In comparison, our Own checkpoint after 20 updates retains coefficients near one along each component's original direction and pairwise angles ranging from 88.04 to 92.41 degrees; its exported BF16 student's increment has 4.90% outside energy, versus 10.52% for the sum of saved FP32 components. The same checkpoint achieves a 32.72% macro score across six benchmarks, compared with 32.44% for its initial task-arithmetic merge. These measurements describe geometry and capability at the evaluated training stages and settings.

Audited Gradients, Anchored Policies: Model-Based Reinforcement Learning for Efficient Flow Control

Ruige Kong, Stefania Fresca

Feedback controllers for flows are expensive to design, since each candidate must be evaluated with a high-fidelity solver. Differentiable surrogates reduce that cost, but they also introduce a failure mode that prediction error cannot detect. Policy optimization uses the derivative of the objective with respect to the action, which we call the action gradient and which can be computed either through the surrogate or through the high-fidelity solver. Optimization through the surrogate uses only its action gradient, and a surrogate can follow the flow trajectory closely while misestimating this derivative. The surrogate action gradient is compared against a central finite-difference estimate from the high-fidelity solver on held-out states, under acceptance criteria fixed before the comparison, and we call that comparison an audit. A low-dimensional feedback law is then fitted to the surrogate action gradient when the surrogate passes the audit, and to the solver estimate when it does not. This law initializes a neural policy, and a quadratic penalty holds the policy near it during optimization. The proposed approach is validated on four test cases. The resulting controllers outperform or match a model-free baseline, while using 3 to 156 times fewer high-fidelity solver interactions across the four test cases. One surrogate predicts the objective at correlation 0.9996 but its action gradient at only 0.459, so prediction accuracy does not identify a usable surrogate. Another surrogate, whose action gradient agrees with the solver estimate at correlation 0.996, makes the objective 0.52% worse when the policy is optimized through it without the anchor penalty, so an accurate gradient alone does not provide a working controller. Finally, a surrogate that passes the audit at one fixed action fails it around the actions the deployed controller applies, so a pass is local.

M-PFT: Angular Margin for Calibrated Prompt-Tuning of Fine-Tuned Vision-Language Models

Shihab Aaqil Ahamed, Mohamed Ansaff, Nang Khin Myat Lay Phyu, Udaya Sampath K. Perera Miriya Thanthrige, Chamira U. S. Edussooriya, Ranga Rodrigo, Muhammad Haris Khan

Prompt-tuning has emerged as a parameter-efficient technique for adapting large-scale vision-language models (VLMs) to downstream tasks while keeping the pre-trained weights frozen. However, prompt-tuned VLMs often suffer from a base-to-novel dual miscalibration problem, where models typically show underconfidence on base classes and overconfidence on novel classes. Current VLM calibration approaches address this dual miscalibration either separately or jointly, but lack a geometric understanding of the hyperspherical embedding space and thus fail to exploit angular discriminative properties to adjust distorted decision boundaries. To address this, we propose M-PFT, a novel angular-margin approach for prompt tuning that introduces two complementary objectives to promote class discriminability and semantic anchoring. This enlarges the angular margin by 1) minimizing the maximum cosine similarity between tunable class embeddings to reduce embedding collapse and 2) minimizing the angular distance between each tunable embedding and its corresponding zero-shot embedding to preserve semantic geometry. Extensive experiments across 9 prompt-tuning methods with various backbones on 15 diverse datasets show that our approach reduces the average calibration error on novel classes, while remaining competitive on base classes and maintaining comparable accuracy. Further analyses reveal that M-PFT effectively adjusts distorted decision boundaries induced by prompt tuning while preserving the pre-trained embedding space, improving calibration with negligible computational overhead.

Agent-Wise Test-Time Normalization for Camera-Based Cooperative Perception with Corrupted Collaborators

Yu Wang, Shaohua Wang, Yicheng Li, Kecheng Sun

Intermediate-fusion cooperative perception lets a vehicle detect objects beyond its own field of view by fusing features shared by nearby agents. When only a collaborator's cameras are degraded by fog, motion blur or exposure errors, the features it shares drift away from the training distribution while the ego input stays clean, and cooperation can become worse than not cooperating at all. Test-time batch-normalization adaptation, the standard training-free remedy, estimates statistics over the pooled batch of all agents and therefore normalizes both the clean ego and the corrupted collaborator with a mixture of two distributions. We show that this mixing harms even the clean ego: when the ego shares BN statistics with a corrupted collaborator, its detection from its own features drops by 3.58 to 6.68 AP@0.5 points although its cameras are untouched. We propose AgentNorm, an agent-wise test-time normalization that i) estimates the normalization statistics of every collaborator from its own current observation and ii) anchors the ego to its source statistics, so that the ego path remains exactly the original model. AgentNorm has no learnable parameters, needs no gradients or labels, is independent of the order of test frames, and runs locally on each agent without changing the transmitted features. On camera-based OPV2V with four fusion checkpoints and collaborator-only corruptions, AgentNorm raises AP@0.5 under severity-5 fog from 29.42-37.25 to 43.32-58.86 and outperforms pooled normalization by 5.53-12.94 points under fog and motion blur, at an extra latency of 2.3 ms per frame. On the real-world DAIR-V2X-C dataset, it outperforms pooled normalization in all ten infrastructure-corruption settings. We also report the cases it does not fix: occlusion, which removes image content rather than shifting feature statistics, and the case in which all agents share the same shift.

Sparse Fine-Tuning for Parameter-Efficient Adversarial Training

Zhaoxin Wang, Zihang Ding, Handing Wang

Deep neural networks achieve strong performance across many tasks but remain vulnerable to adversarial perturbations. Adversarial training (AT) is one of the most effective defenses, yet it suffers from high memory cost on large models and the persistent clean-robust accuracy trade-off. Recent work introduces parameter-efficient fine-tuning, such as LoRA, into AT, but this constraint induces a notable robustness gap relative to full-parameter AT. In this work, we revisit parameter-efficient adversarial fine-tuning from a parameter space perspective. Through gradient analysis, we find that adversarial optimization is highly concentrated on a small subset of parameters, which we call Robustness-Critical Parameters (RCPs), suggesting that robustness is encoded in a sparse subspace. Building on this observation, we propose Sparse Adversarial Fine-Tuning (SAFT), which identifies RCPs via adversarial gradient saliency and performs AT by updating only these parameters while freezing the rest. Across architectures and datasets, SAFT outperforms LoRA-based methods in both clean and robust accuracy with fewer trainable parameters, and approaches or surpasses full parameter AT in robustness while updating only about 5\% of parameters.

Anatomical Prior-Guided Routing for 3D Lung Tumor Segmentation

Uihyeon Maeng, Jonghun Kim, Hyunjin Park

Accurate lung tumor segmentation in computed tomography (CT) is essential for treatment planning, response assessment, and quantitative imaging analysis. However, 3D lung tumor segmentation remains challenging because tumors occupy only a small portion of the whole volume and exhibit heterogeneous appearances across anatomical locations. In patch-based training and sliding-window inference for 3D medical image segmentation, location-dependent heterogeneity is further amplified because cropped image patches provide limited global anatomical context, making it difficult for a model to adapt its feature processing according to the anatomical region from which each patch is extracted. To address this limitation, we propose an Anatomical Prior-guided Routing Module (APRM) for lung tumor segmentation. For each image patch, we obtain anatomical prior information of regional occupancy and deterministically select an anatomical routing gate. The selected gate then performs sparse feature routing over a shared expert adapter pool, enabling anatomy-specific feature adaptation while sharing expert capacity across regions. We evaluate APRM on two datasets with various backbone networks and demonstrate consistent improvements over baseline models. Furthermore, feature visualizations show that APRM produces different feature responses depending on patch location, indicating that the proposed routing mechanism effectively adapts feature processing according to anatomical context.

Data Scaling Laws in Stereo Matching: From In-domain Fitting to Cross-domain Generalization

Tong Zhao, Di Liang, Yiming Zeng, Bo Xu, Yingzhi Tang, Chen Bai

In the era of large models, stereo matching is developing towards generalizable foundation models trained on million-scale synthetic images. However, how model performance scales up with synthetic data is still underexplored. In this work, we preliminarily investigate the in-domain and cross-domain data scaling laws in stereo matching. A realistic synthetic dataset, named XStereo, containing 200 thousand samples is first built to serve as a high-quality benchmark for data scaling research. In-domain training on five large-scale datasets demonstrate a two-regime scaling behavior with data, transitioning from the standard power law to saturation, where the critical transition point is approximately invariant under a fixed model. Cross-domain performance exhibits a substantially weaker and less predictable dependence on the source-domain data scale. The distribution and coverage diversity are more effective for diminishing domain gaps. More instructive suggestions are provided for high-efficiency data construction and training. The insightful findings are significant verification and extension of the existing scaling laws in both language and vision, paving the way to efficient and stronger stereo matching. The XStereo dataset is public at https://huggingface.co/datasets/Tong-Zhao/XStereo.

RoSAMDepth: Robust Self-supervised Depth Estimation Leveraging Segment Anything Model

Xuanang Gao, Zhiwei Ning, Gengming Zhang, Jiaxi Cao, Runze Yang, Zhonglong Zheng, Jie Yang, Rong Xiao, Wei Liu

Robust depth estimation aims to maintain high-quality depths across diverse conditions. However, most existing methods estimate depth without taking into account the object-level information. As a result, the predicted depth may easily deviate within objects and become blurred under adverse conditions. To overcome this weakness, we propose RoSAMDepth, a novel framework that can assist robust self-supervised depth estimation in leveraging rich and diverse object-level priors from the Segment Anything Model (SAM). We focus on incorporating object-level information across three key aspects: a segment-guided representation contrasting method that injects object-level awareness into the feature representation space; an adaptive regional outlier masking strategy combined with a regional Gaussian likelihood loss that enforces regional depth smoothness; and an object-level reliability estimation strategy that mitigates the influence of unreliable supervision. Extensive experiments across multiple datasets and diverse weather conditions demonstrate that our method produces sharper, more accurate depth predictions, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art methods. Our code is available at https://github.com/xagao/RoSAMDepth.

Self-Consistency Geometry Injection for Multi-view Consistent Image Generation

Yupeng Xiao, Yankai Wu, Jianjie Luo, Jiajie Lin, Yi Yu, Zhenguo Yang

Recent diffusion models have achieved impressive progress in single image to multi-view generation by synthesizing consistent novel views efficiently. Existing methods usually explicitly or implicitly couple geometry inference and texture synthesis, making generation vulnerable to inaccurate geometry prediction. Although several geometry-guided methods have explored decoupling, they usually treat predicted geometry as a fixed condition and do not explicitly model whether the geometry is reliable. We therefore propose Reliability-Guided Multi-View Generation (RGMV), which comprises a parameter-free Self-Consistency Geometry Gate (SCG) and a Reliability-aware Geometry Injection (RGI) module. Specifically, a geometry proposal generator produces multiple geometry predictions for the same reference image and target viewpoints under different stochastic sampling configurations. SCG measures their spatial agreement to derive a reliability map, which RGI uses to modulate the injection strength of geometry-conditioned residuals in the RGB synthesis model. Moreover, RGMV can adapt to different geometry proposal generators. As a result, RGMV preserves stronger geometric guidance in stable regions while attenuating unreliable geometry cues before they propagate into multi-view RGB generation. Extensive experiments show that reliability-aware geometry injection improves the geometric quality of multi-view generation. Code: https://github.com/WISLab-GDUT/RGMV.

Component Translation and Judge-Labelled Refusal: A Multilingual Safety Evaluation

Yongxi Zhou, Junwei Yao, Wenbo Ye, Jiahui Ge

Whole-prompt translation evaluates a request in different languages but does not compare which component carries a language. We introduce a paired reversal: translate the complete request component or the complete operation component into the same target language, holding behaviour, English frame and translated-span count fixed. Component identity and non-English text quantity change together. Assigning English, Chinese and Spanish independently to the two components yields nine configurations on 190 of 300 sampled harmful behaviours retained after consensus annotation and automatic gating. Refusal scored by language-model judges is the primary outcome; unsafe compliance is reported separately. On Gemini 2.5 Flash and DeepSeek, three refusal swaps are about 5.3 percentage points and the fourth is -0.5; none survives the pre-specified six-test Holm correction. In an exploratory extension, Mistral Small 4 has a 13.7-point Spanish swap (95% interval [8.4, 18.9]), which survives the twelve-test Holm sensitivity correction under the primary judge, and 12.1 points under a second judge. GPT-5.4-mini estimates are within 2 points of zero. Component assignment supplies an additional evaluation dimension. Evaluations should record component languages and translated text quantity, comparing matched behaviours at equal translated-span count while interpreting refusal and unsafe compliance separately.

CARF: Context-Aware Reasoning Supervision for Scientific Evidence Screening

Nishtha Agarwal, Adam Visokay, Astha Wahi, Kwabena Owusu Aninkora, Amy Gu, Tyler McCormick, Li Liu

Systematic reviews synthesize biomedical evidence to inform clinical and public-health decision-making, but require domain experts to manually screen thousands of studies against detailed eligibility criteria. We investigate whether language models can assist with this bottleneck by learning from expert feedback on title and abstract screening decisions and model-generated reasoning. We introduce the Context-Aware Reasoning Framework (CARF), a human-in-the-loop framework that uses expert feedback on model-generated reasoning to adapt language models to context-dependent screening tasks. We evaluate CARF on systematic reviews of verbal autopsy and cause-of-death ascertainment methods used in global public health mortality surveillance. CARF shows competitive screening performance compared to evaluated models while retaining high sensitivity, and is more inclined to retain ambiguous, context-dependent studies for human review. Together, these findings suggest that expert-corrected reasoning is a promising supervision signal for context-dependent scientific screening, while retaining a role for human oversight.

Beyond Aggregate WER: In-domain Adaptation, Learner Speech, Code-Switching, and Speaker Attribution in Online Spanish Learning Classrooms

Olga Kellert, Samhitha Harish, Johnatan E Bonilla, Nemika Tyagi, Phanos Kareen, Nelvin Licona-Guevara

Automatic speech recognition (ASR) increasingly supplies transcripts for educational NLP, but aggregate word error rate (WER) can conceal errors concentrated in learner speech, multilingual turns, and speaker attribution. We compare platform-integrated, open-weight, adapted, and commercial ASR configurations on held-out sessions of online L2 Spanish interaction between L1 English learners and their instructors and tutors. Because English is the learner's shared first language, the interactions contain Spanish-only, English-only, and code-switched turns. Evaluation combines overall WER and character error rate (CER), WER disaggregated by speaker role and language context, and diarization error rate (DER). We also adapt Whisper large-v3 and MOSS-Transcribe-Diarize with LoRA using human-corrected in-domain classroom data. MOSS adaptation produced consistent improvements across speaker roles and language contexts, whereas Whisper showed smaller gains. Domain match proved more important than data volume: Larger out-of-domain bilingual and learner corpora did not outperform the smaller classroom corpus, where learner speech and code-switching are embedded in instructor--learner interaction. The open-weight MOSS adapter was competitive with commercial Muse Voice Transcribe on WER and CER and achieved lower system-provided DER on the recordings eligible for each system. Learner, English-only, and code-switched speech remained more difficult than instructor and Spanish-only speech. The findings demonstrate the value of condition-aware evaluation and interactionally matched data for bilingual educational ASR.

Large Language Models for Accurate Medical Chart Abstraction: Enabling Scalable and Secure Artificial Intelligence Deployment in Stroke

Zhusi Zhong, Carl M. Porto, David Hong, Miranda Gonzales, Revyn Kim, Gnaneswari Karayi, Lulu Bi, Joshua R. Feler, Elias Shaaya, Scott Collins, Liqi Shu, Grayson Baird, Mahesh Jayaraman, Shadi Yaghi, Zhicheng Jiao, Dylan N. Wolman

Medical chart abstraction transforms unstructured clinical narratives into structured variables for research and quality monitoring. We evaluated a prompting-based large language model (LLM) framework for extracting eight clinical variables from 2,416 neurointerventional acute stroke intervention reports across three hospitals. Twenty-two instruction-tuned open-source LLMs were evaluated across architectures, model sizes, and biomedical adaptations using Quick Response and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. Non-expert staff annotations served as the cross-model reference standard. Evaluation was restricted to report-variable pairs with a valid LLaMA3.3-70B CoT prediction and a corresponding reference label. Performance measures included extraction accuracy, inference latency, and agreement with expert adjudication. Twenty discordant cases per variable underwent review by two senior physicians and four medical students. LLaMA3.3-70B achieved the highest overall accuracy (94.8%). CoT improved performance on inference-dependent variables such as site of occlusion, while Quick Response was optimal for directly stated procedural fields. Expert adjudication showed that AI predictions were more closely aligned with expert interpretations than non-expert annotations for most variables, with intracranial angioplasty remaining a challenge. Locally deployed LLMs can support scalable clinical information extraction and retrospective stroke registry curation, with expert oversight for ambiguous cases.

Active Constraints for Simultaneous Guidance and Safety in Handheld Micromanipulation

Roger Ho, Robert A. MacLachlan, Cameron N. Riviere

In micromanipulation, erroneous hand motion limits unaided manipulation accuracy, which can affect task performance and safety in critical applications like microsurgery. Handheld robots (micromanipulators) enable motion accuracy beyond human limits by providing robotic assistance in a low-cost, compact, intuitive form factor. Particularly, active constraints are an effective form of robotic assistance, which aids the operator by restricting the robot's permissible motion based on task-specific requirements or objectives, thereby providing task guidance and enforcing safety. However, existing formulations of active constraints for handheld systems are limited, most notably because of the inability to provide guidance and enforce safety simultaneously. Implementing active constraints with optimization-based methods can address these limitations, but existing implementations are designed for free-standing robots, and neglect that handheld robots actuate relative to the operator's hand with a limited workspace. In this work, we propose an optimization-based active constraint framework for handheld micromanipulation that enables simultaneous guidance and safety enforcement. The formulation was implemented in a handheld robot and demonstrated on a medically relevant artificial task. Results show that the proposed formulation reduced safety violation rate by more than two orders of magnitude relative to baselines, while recovering the threefold accuracy loss from enforcing safety alone.

3D Occupancy-Language Fields from mmWave Radar

Haowen Lai, Zhiwei Zheng, Mingmin Zhao

Radar systems can recover scene geometry and recognize predefined categories, but these outputs do not provide a persistent spatial representation for open-ended language queries. Such queries require linking observations from different viewpoints to the same physical locations. We present TextRadar, a framework that constructs a scene-level 3D occupancy-language field from panoramic radar observations. The field combines occupancy with scale-dependent language features at surface locations, allowing the reconstructed scene to be queried with new descriptions. A single-frame radar distillation model predicts aligned ranges and language features from each radar observation. Using radar-estimated poses, we register these predictions to reconstruct the field from radar alone in a new scene. Experiments on approximately 20k synchronized observations across seven buildings demonstrate scene-level natural-language querying and prompt-free semantic visualization. Analysis of camera-to-radar concept transferability shows stronger agreement for structures, materials, and human poses than for colors and printed text.

Propagating Elevation-Map Uncertainty Through the Contact Maximum in Closed Form

Aleš Kučera, Karel Zimmermann

Risk-aware planners score paths on uncertain elevation maps using the path cost's mean and standard deviation. Modeling rigid contact, however, requires computing a maximum over several uncertain cells. First-order propagation loses accuracy here by differentiating at only a single cell, while Monte Carlo sampling requires a full path evaluation per draw. We compute the moments of that contact maximum in closed form using Clark's pairwise recursion. By tracking each contact's covariance against the shared map cells, we propagate the smooth remainder using exact Gaussian quadratic-form identities. A contest-depth calibration, fitted once on two design traverses, closes the aggregate standard-deviation shortfall that remains. On 317 held-out rover path segments, scored against a Monte Carlo reference from the same belief, every pre-registered criterion was met. The corrected Clark fold cuts the median error of the mean from linearization's 2.3% to 0.19% and attains the lowest error in the conditional value at risk (CVaR) at the 90% level of every method tested. A benchmark plan costs just 5.5 microseconds on a GPU. These accuracy gains concentrate at contested contacts. While they seldom change which path is chosen on this terrain, the fold still selects the reference-best path in 98% of decisions against linearization's 93 to 96%. On a second dataset the mean transfers, though the risk number does not.

P3: Persistent Particle Planning for Constrained Diffusion Control

Hikmet Simsir, Mahyar Fardinfar, Ozgur S. Oguz

Diffusion models provide expressive priors over trajectories, but adapting these priors to test-time constraints requires maintaining feasibility and consistency across successive control decisions. We introduce Persistent Particle Planning (P3), a sequential Monte Carlo framework for diffusion control that maintains a weighted population of candidate plans across replanning steps. At each control step, P3 shifts and partially re-noises the candidate trajectories, refines them under the latest observation, and uses constraint-aware weighting and resampling to select among alternative continuations without retraining the diffusion model. We consider denoising and replanning as one Feynman--Kac particle system and analyze it under an idealized repair. We prove that the re-noising depth controls how reliably a kept plan stays on its route, and that keeping a rare, well-separated route takes far fewer plans than rediscovering it by sampling from scratch. Experiments under multiple test-time constraint configurations show that population reuse reduces route switching and improves success without constraint violations. Because P3 refines earlier plans instead of redrawing them, it also needs fewer denoising iterations per replan. On maze-navigation tasks, it plans faster than both regenerated populations and methods that correct a single sampled plan by constrained optimization. Code and pretrained models are available at https://github.com/p3-username/p3-anon.

How (and How Not) to Use Data Augmentation in VLA Post-Training

Bram Grooten, Joaquin Vanschoren

Vision-language-action (VLA) models currently demonstrate strong performance in a wide range of real-world robotics tasks. However, they often still lack the generalization ability to handle large visual out-of-distribution shifts. Post-training of VLAs with reinforcement learning (RL) has been shown to benefit robustness, but significant room for improvement remains. In this work, we systematically study the effect of image augmentation on VLA post-training. We find that it is crucial to augment only the critic module during RL updates, while leaving the actor's input clean during both rollouts and updates. For $π_{0.5}$ and GR00T N1.5 this raises out-of-distribution success on LIBERO-Plus by $7.8$ and $10.0$ points respectively, while augmenting the actor collapses training entirely. We investigate a range of augmentation types and strengths, and provide practical recommendations for improving generalization in VLA post-training.

ReMaD: Tuning-free Domain Adaptation for Classification and Out-of-Distribution Detection

Elijah Bolluyt, Cristina Comaniciu

We introduce Reduced-rank Mahalanobis Distance (ReMaD), a novel prototypical distance-based refinement to classification and out-of-distribution (OOD) detection using pretrained models without finetuning. We use embeddings of the target dataset to fit closed-form distribution statistics in the model's latent space which can classify in-distribution samples and detect OOD samples, all without training or prior knowledge of the OOD data. Building on prototype classification and OOD detection, we analyze the distribution properties of large pretrained models when processing new datasets; based on this analysis, we formulate a simple modification to Mahalanobis Distance to adapt models' latent space distributions to new domains by removing unused features, without the finetuning or hyperparameter searches required by other adaptation procedures. We demonstrate the efficacy of this method to adapt existing large pretrained image embedding models to new classification domains outside their trained capabilities by testing across four target datasets, with competitive performance in both classification and OOD detection.

Spacecraft Rendezvous Trajectory Generation with Modular Constraints via Diffusion Model Composition

Mariko A. Storey-Matsutani, Richard Linares

Emerging mission classes such as on-orbit servicing, satellite inspection, and active debris removal require trajectory design methods that are adaptable to a variety of mission scenarios. We present a diffusion-based trajectory generation approach for rendezvous and proximity operations (RPO) that enables flexible configuration of mission constraints. First, individual energy-based diffusion models are trained to satisfy distinct constraints such as approach cone and sensor line-of-sight from a set of optimized trajectories. Then, at inference time, the learned energy models can be composed with one another, or with an analytically defined energy field, to enforce specific constraint combinations. We validate this framework with the composition of a learned approach cone model and a learned sensor line-of-sight model, as well as a learned approach cone model and synthetic obstacle avoidance model, both of which yield constraint satisfaction rates that are within 1 percentage point of the single-constraint models or higher. These results indicate that our compositional diffusion framework can provide a modular approach to RPO trajectory design and enable reconfiguration for new constraint combinations without requiring model retraining.

Three-dimensional gait analysis guides rehabilitation after spinal cord injury but depends on marker-based motion capture and force plates, which few clinics have. Deep Prior Learning for Embodied Perception

Yimou Wu, Jiaxin Guo, Yun-hui Liu, Zheng Li

Embodied systems need geometric perception that exploits available observations beyond images alone. Recent feed-forward 3D models incorporate geometric priors, including camera poses, intrinsics, and depth. However, handling noisy poses, preserving accurate priors, and recovering physical scale require more than simply accepting these inputs. We introduce \emph{Vision-Prior Geometry Grounded Transformer} (VPGGT), a VGGT-based framework that extends OmniVGGT for prior-aware embodied perception. We formulate sensor-motivated pose corruptions from ground-truth trajectories for training and introduce a parameter-free \emph{prior residual connection} (PRC) to mitigate \emph{prior dilution}, where predictions are less accurate than their supplied pose priors. Our noise formulation targets camera poses; supplied intrinsics and depth receive no additional corruption. We further introduce \emph{Metric Global Attention}, which conditions a global scale token on available pose and depth scales and predicts a shared metric scaling factor for the geometric outputs. Experiments across four datasets show that \emph{PRC} improves translation-direction accuracy and joint pose AUC over a matched training baseline when camera priors are provided for all views, under both exact and corrupted poses. These results support explicit prior access during refinement as a useful addition to feature-level conditioning.
